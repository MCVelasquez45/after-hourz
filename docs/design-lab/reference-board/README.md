# Reference Board — After Hourz Design Lab

> A curated study of external sites whose execution proves (or cautions against) the
> **"Booth Light"** thesis. This board extracts transferable *principles*, never surface style.
> Per project rule (`docs/12`, CLAUDE.md): **extract principles from great work; never copy.**

## Purpose

After Hourz is a premium automotive craft brand — body work, custom paint, restoration, and custom
vehicle craftsmanship. The digital experience must read as **craftsmanship · transformation ·
material · light · paint · chrome · precision · restraint · cinematic storytelling**. This board
collects the strongest external evidence for how those qualities are achieved on screen, so the
three prototype directions (**Chrome Heritage**, **Booth Light**, **After Dark**) can be argued
from proof rather than taste.

Each reference is judged for what it teaches *us*, and — just as important — what it would poison us
with if copied. The board is a filter, not a mood wall.

## Methodology

- **Render before judging.** References are studied as executed experiences (composition, motion,
  spacing, mobile behavior), not as static thumbnails or brand lore.
- **Principle, not pixels.** For every reference we record a single *Principle To Apply* and an
  explicit *What NOT To Copy*. A reference earns its place only if the principle survives being
  stripped of the source's brand equity.
- **Direction-mapped.** Every reference is tagged to the prototype direction(s) it most serves:
  `chrome-heritage`, `booth-light`, `after-dark`, or `all`.
- **Guardrails enforced.** Anything that pulls toward forbidden drift — motorsports/OEM coldness,
  fintech/SaaS gradients and tech-UI, EV/consumer-electronics clinical lighting, neon/cyberpunk, or
  fabricated/stock imagery presented as real work — is flagged, not adopted. (See CLAUDE.md, `P8`
  asset-truth rule.)
- **Truth over aspiration.** No reference justifies dressing external or supercar imagery as
  Anthony's completed work. All portfolio imagery must be his real, shot work.

## Synthesis

### Principles To Apply To After Hourz

1. **Build a rhythm of darkness and controlled light, not a stack of sections.** BMW M, Ferrari,
   Linear, and Rimac all prove that alternating a near-black stage with a clean bright gallery — and
   letting *photographic lighting* create all depth — is the elevation itself. Ban drop-shadows; the
   lit subject in a near-black frame is Booth Light. Use black↔light section inversion as a rhythm
   device to reset the eye between chapters.

2. **Single-accent discipline: color as punctuation, never decoration.** Ferrari (one Rosso note per
   section), Vercel (monochrome base then one color punch), and PORSCHEvolution (deep black + one
   heritage accent + neutral gray) converge on the same rule. Pick ONE saturated event — a deep
   automotive paint hue or warm chrome-glint — and let it appear once per section as a CTA, rule, or
   numeral. When real paint color appears, it should land with maximum impact.

3. **Object worship: the finished vehicle is the product.** Apple, Rimac, Scout, and Kevin Valete
   all make one object the hero and let negative space do the persuading. Treat Anthony's finished
   build with reverent lighting and enough air that the work sells itself. Let **size and air** carry
   hierarchy so copy stays quiet and confident.

4. **Systematize the portfolio so a mixed fleet reads curated.** Porsche's fixed aspect ratio and
   repeated card proportion are what separate "gallery" from "catalog." Define ONE image ratio and
   one card proportion, then repeat it across cars, trucks, and bikes.

5. **Prove premium through reduction and stillness.** Polestar (text-link CTAs, motionless hero) and
   Terminal Industries (rationed reveals with quiet beats between them) show that restraint reads as
   expensive. Ration motion hard — every reveal must answer "reveal? orient? story of the finish?"
   Confidence of stillness beats effect-stacking.

6. **Commit to ONE signature motion device.** Stripe (its gradient), Lando Norris (its tracked
   object), and Active Theory (its continuous morph) all win by relentless commitment to a single
   device while everything else stays quiet. For After Hourz that device is a **continuous
   real-light sweep / specular highlight travelling across chrome and wet clearcoat** as scroll
   advances, revealing paint depth. It must reveal real material, not perform.

7. **Weave a true human/heritage narrative into the dark frame.** McLaren gives its maker's story
   first-class placement alongside the products. Anthony's story and the visible hand of craft
   deserve an editorial "behind the work" band — authored, not templated — and it must be TRUE.

8. **Make scroll perform the craft story.** PORSCHEvolution and Terminal Industries use scroll as
   narrative time: descend from raw metal to finished paint/chrome so scrolling literally enacts the
   transformation — primer → block-sand → base → clear → chrome, one deliberate reveal per beat.

9. **Each signature section is one cinematic frame.** SpaceX and Kevin Valete prove full-bleed,
   one-image-per-viewport discipline (type as a quiet label, black as connective void). Use it for
   signature moments — not everywhere — so it never becomes scroll-jacking.

10. **Editorial-monograph layout for the portfolio.** By-Kin and Obys show that a characterful-but-
    legible headline face, asymmetric grid, one dominant element per view, and dramatic full-bleed/
    inset crop contrast make work read like a printed monograph rather than a uniform thumbnail grid.

11. **Govern the type system.** Vercel's strict three-weight discipline (each weight with a role) and
    Apple's size-and-air hierarchy prevent the "too many weights" amateur tell. Let scale and
    spacing carry hierarchy; keep typeface variety near zero.

12. **Continuous, weighted, invisible motion.** By-Kin and Active Theory set the bar: transitions
    settle into stillness and survive a frame-by-frame look. Keep easing mechanical
    (`cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1)`), settling like weighted machinery; degrade gracefully under
    `prefers-reduced-motion` (morphs → ≤120ms fades; hero pinned still).

### Failure Patterns To Avoid

- **Motorsports / OEM drift.** Ferrari's Rosso Corsa and F1 numerals, BMW's tricolor stripe, McLaren's
  supercar aspiration, Lando Norris's F1 framing — all explicitly forbidden brand drift. Take the
  discipline; reject the racing glamour and the "engineered/factory" coldness. We are *finished by
  hand*, not built on a line.

- **Fintech / SaaS tells.** Stripe's animated candy gradient, Vercel's aggressively negative-tracked
  "minified" headlines, the geometric-sans + tight-tracking voice, and UI-screenshot showcases would
  instantly make the site "SaaS with automotive words." After Hourz has no product UI — its
  screenshots are paint and metal. Keep restraint discipline; reject the gradient, the tech-UI
  imagery, and the fintech color story.

- **Consumer-electronics / EV clinical lighting.** Apple's cool showroom-white and Polestar's
  driverless Scandinavian sterility strip paint of its depth, gloss, and controlled-darkness soul.
  Automotive finish needs **warm, raking, reflective light** that shows the clearcoat, not flat
  product-catalog illumination. Take the method (object + whitespace + pacing); reject the lighting.

- **Neon / cyberpunk darkness.** Linear's darkness is cool and digital; ours must feel like a lit
  paint booth — warm reflections on wet clearcoat, not backlit glass. Never let dark slide toward
  neon/cyberpunk (a forbidden mutation).

- **Synthetic glow and abstraction over real material.** Linear's synthetic gradients/glass panels,
  Vercel's technical illustration, and Stripe's parallelogram abstractions all substitute invented
  graphics for photography. After Hourz lives or dies on real images of metal, paint, and reflection.

- **WebGL-by-default and effect overload.** Active Theory's particle FX / multiplayer spectacle and
  Scout's full-CGI configurator machinery violate the HTML-first, WebGL-by-exception rule and "motion
  communicates or it's cut." No spinning particle fields. Active Theory's 6.40/10 accessibility is a
  ceiling we must not inherit.

- **Auto-advancing carousels & scroll-jacking.** McLaren's auto-advancing hero and the literal
  one-viewport scroll-snap everywhere create focus/accessibility problems and bury content. Use
  scene-per-viewport only for signature moments.

- **Interchangeable-studio blankness.** By-Kin's cool-minimal neutrality could sit on any creative
  portfolio. After Hourz must feel like grease, metal, and controlled light — not a design studio.

- **Full monochrome & silent type.** Kevin Valete's B&W would delete the brand's core material (paint
  depth, custom color, chrome iridescence), and a shop needs more voice than a silent photo
  portfolio.

- **Museum / theme-park conceits.** PORSCHEvolution's audio-sync and decades-timeline device, and any
  "mission" vocabulary (SpaceX), only earn their place with genuine narrative need. A working shop's
  site is about the work, not a walk-through.

- **Fabricated or borrowed portfolio.** Across every reference, the hardest rule (P8): never present
  stock, rendered, or supercar imagery as Anthony's completed work. Only his real, shot work.
