## Premium Automotive

### BMW M

- **URL:** https://www.bmw-m.com/en/index.html
- **Screen / Section:** Full-viewport hero band + alternating dark/light content rhythm
- **What Works:** The single cleanest execution of our exact thesis. A near-black canvas where full-bleed automotive photography is lit against darkness like a car in a showroom, then the page deliberately alternates deep-dark hero bands with clean bright content areas to create a cinematic breathing rhythm. Depth comes entirely from photographic light-vs-dark contrast, not from shadows or effects. The M tricolor appears sparingly as a single brand signature note. This is 'Booth Light' already proven at scale: controlled light does the elevation work, restraint does the rest.
- **Composition:** Full-width black hero band, h1 left-aligned over the photograph with a small subtitle beneath, generous grid-aligned spacing (~96px between major bands, ~64px inside hero photo bands). Composition is confident and left-weighted rather than centered-and-hedging.
- **Typography:** Proprietary BMWTypeNextLatin. Massive uppercase display headings set in a Light weight (300) — the tension of huge scale at low weight reads as precision, not shouting. Regular weight for body/UI. Hierarchy is scale-driven, not typeface-variety-driven.
- **Image Strategy:** Full-bleed cinematic vehicle photography as the primary structural element; the car is the light source in a dark frame. Photography carries all visual weight; type stays quiet over it.
- **Motion:** Cinematic scroll rhythm from the dark/light band alternation itself rather than heavy animation; the reveal is compositional (enter dark, land on the lit subject) so it survives motion-off.
- **Spacing:** Generous, strictly grid-aligned. ~96px between bands, ~64px interior. Space is a material, not leftover.
- **Geometry:** The most angular system studied — sharp corners exclusively, every element a precise rectangle, 0px radius CTAs with 1px outlines. Reads as engineered precision.
- **Mobile Behavior:** Dark bands stack; full-bleed photography reflows to portrait; left-aligned headline hierarchy holds because it never depended on centering.
- **Principle To Apply:** Build the page as a rhythm of darkness and controlled light, not a stack of sections. Let photographic lighting create all depth — ban drop-shadows; the lit subject in a near-black frame IS the elevation. This is literally Booth Light and we should treat BMW M as the north-star proof.
- **What NOT To Copy:** The tricolor stripe (motorsports signature — forbidden brand drift for us) and the hard-uppercase display register can read cold/corporate. We want warmth of hand-craft, not factory. Do not import the racing/OEM coldness; swap the 'engineered' feeling for 'finished by hand.'
- **Relevant Direction:** `after-dark`

### Ferrari

- **URL:** https://www.ferrari.com/en-EN
- **Screen / Section:** Full-bleed cinematic hero + chiaroscuro section alternation
- **What Works:** Ferrari reads as cinematic editorial — a luxury-magazine spread, not a car-OEM site. Its 'chiaroscuro rhythm' alternates a deep ink stage with a paper-white gallery, and the one saturated color (Rosso Corsa) appears exactly once per section as a single decisive note — a CTA, a rule, a numeral. This is the masterclass in disciplined accent: color as punctuation, never decoration. High-contrast photography (car against black sky, paddock light, Maranello tarmac) does the emotional lifting while type stays deliberately quiet.
- **Composition:** Full-bleed hero photograph fills the viewport top; type sits quiet beneath rather than fighting the image. Sections are editorial spreads with clear ink-vs-paper staging.
- **Typography:** FerrariSans at modest weights — display 500, body 400, never bombastic. CTA labels uppercase with ~1.4px tracking. The restraint is the sophistication: the brand is confident enough to whisper.
- **Image Strategy:** Full-bleed cinematic frames — car details, livery panels, trackside moments — carry all visual weight. High-contrast, single-light-source feel that matches our booth aesthetic.
- **Motion:** Interactive cinematic transitions between chiaroscuro stages; the dark-to-light alternation is itself the choreography, so the story survives with motion reduced.
- **Spacing:** Magazine-grade — generous margins, editorial column discipline, each spread given room to be one idea.
- **Geometry:** Editorial rectangles and rules; a single red hairline/numeral as geometric accent rather than ornament.
- **Mobile Behavior:** Hero photography optimized to portrait; the quiet-type-under-image relationship scales cleanly because type was never load-bearing over the image.
- **Principle To Apply:** Adopt the single-accent discipline: pick ONE saturated color (for us, a deep automotive paint hue or a warm chrome-glint, NOT Ferrari red) and let it appear once per section as punctuation — a CTA, a rule, a numeral — never as fill or theme. And treat the page as editorial chiaroscuro: ink stage, paper gallery, alternating.
- **What NOT To Copy:** Rosso Corsa itself and any F1/motorsports numerals or livery language — that is racing-brand territory and forbidden drift for us. The heritage-luxury weight is Ferrari's earned equity; we must earn our own through visible craft, not borrow theirs.
- **Relevant Direction:** `all`

### Porsche

- **URL:** https://www.porsche.com/international/
- **Screen / Section:** Modular product grid + neutral studio staging
- **What Works:** Porsche demonstrates disciplined systematization: a modular, vertically-stacked card grid with consistent aspect ratios (5:4) that creates visual rhythm and predictability without monotony. Product photography commands attention against restrained neutral backgrounds; dark vehicle silhouettes contrast against controlled environmental light. The design system underneath enforces proportional type scaling and dark-mode support, so the craft is in the consistency — nothing is arbitrary. This is how you make a MULTI-vehicle portfolio (cars, trucks, bikes for us) feel curated rather than cataloged.
- **Composition:** Featured hero cards up top establish hierarchy, then a consistent rectangular grid of vehicle categories. Generous whitespace around each card prevents congestion and lets each subject breathe.
- **Typography:** Deliberate scale-progression sans-serif hierarchy; product names and section titles are clear entry points, body copy kept low-weight so imagery dominates. Type is a wayfinding system, not a performance.
- **Image Strategy:** Photography as primary design element; consistent aspect ratios create rhythm; dynamic three-quarter and environmental angles. Neutral backgrounds so the vehicle finish is the hero.
- **Motion:** Restrained; relies on grid rhythm and image quality (WebP, quality-tuned) over animation flourish.
- **Spacing:** Generous, card-isolating whitespace; predictable gutters; rhythm from repetition of proportion.
- **Geometry:** Orthogonal rectangular card units, uniform ratios; architectural predictability.
- **Mobile Behavior:** Mobile-first responsive: WebP with quality degradation, card layouts reflow naturally to a single column while maintaining legibility and proportion.
- **Principle To Apply:** Systematize the portfolio: define ONE fixed image aspect ratio and one card proportion, then repeat it so a mixed fleet (cars, trucks, bikes) reads as a curated collection with visual rhythm. Consistency of proportion is what separates 'gallery' from 'catalog.'
- **What NOT To Copy:** The mostly-light/neutral background register is safe-corporate and pulls away from our near-black Booth Light thesis — take the grid discipline, not the bright OEM stage. Also avoid the lifestyle/charging-infrastructure imagery; our subject is the craft and the finish, not the ownership lifestyle.
- **Relevant Direction:** `chrome-heritage`

### Polestar

- **URL:** https://www.polestar.com/us/
- **Screen / Section:** Single-subject studio hero + 2x2 studio grid + inverted dark footer
- **What Works:** The purest lesson in restraint: 'the chrome refuses to compete with the cars.' The hero is one car in three-quarter view on a studio-gray stage — no driver, no road, no motion. CTAs are plain anchored text ('Learn more →') not filled buttons. The whole site reads as a Stockholm gallery, not a marketing page, and it closes with a dark anthracite footer that inverts ink from black to white — a single, confident tonal turn. This proves that near-total reduction reads as premium when the subject and the spacing are strong enough to carry it.
- **Composition:** Left-aligned text block against full-width imagery; alternating left/right positioning to avoid monotony; 2x2 product-card grid repeating one studio treatment; dark footer as a deliberate tonal payoff.
- **Typography:** Polestar Unica ~30px, weight 400, -1.2px letter-spacing — tight tracking at modest weight reads as engineered calm. Strong scale contrast between headline and restrained body; minimal typeface variation.
- **Image Strategy:** Uniform studio treatment across hero and grid — one subject, controlled light, neutral stage. Consistency of lighting IS the brand.
- **Motion:** Effectively none in the hero (no driver, no road, no motion) — the stillness is the statement. Premium with motion fully off (passes P7 by default).
- **Spacing:** Generous vertical rhythm on a visible grid; even module distribution; whitespace as the primary luxury signal.
- **Geometry:** 0px corners, hard orthogonal edges, architectural precision; reductive rectangles only.
- **Mobile Behavior:** Cards stack, navigation collapses, imagery scales; because the layout is grid-honest and type isn't decorative, it reflows without losing its gallery calm.
- **Principle To Apply:** Have the confidence of stillness and text-link CTAs: prove premium through reduction and spacing, not effects. Make CTAs quiet anchored text where appropriate, and end the page on one deliberate tonal inversion (light body to a deep dark footer) as a compositional payoff rather than a decorated block.
- **What NOT To Copy:** Polestar's cool, driverless, near-clinical Scandinavian minimalism can read bloodless — for After Hourz the reduction must still carry human warmth and evidence of the hand (grain, reflection, paint depth, a craftsman's presence). Take the restraint, add the soul; don't ship the EV-showroom sterility.

### Rimac Automobili (hypercar brand site) — Awwwards Honorable Mention

- **URL:** https://www.awwwards.com/sites/rimac-automobili

- **What Works:** A ruthless three-value system — pure black (#000), pure white (#fff), one cold teal accent (#49c5b6) — lets the machined product carry the entire emotional load. Nothing on the page competes with the metal. Motion (GSAP/Three.js/Barba page transitions) reveals and isolates rather than decorates: sections arrive one focal object at a time so the eye is never asked to choose. 'Premium by restraint, not decoration' at brand scale.
- **Composition:** Single dominant object per viewport, floated in a large dark field. Negative space is the frame — it is what signals 'expensive' and 'engineered.' Compositional tension comes from scale contrast: a small precise UI/label set against a very large product mass, plus black-to-white section inversions that reset the eye. Almost no grid clutter; hierarchy is created by isolation rather than borders or panels.

Personality lives in spacing and confidence (small caps / wide tracking for spec labels) rather than in a characterful face. For After Hourz this is a caution as much as a model: it reads engineering-cold; we want warmer craft.
- **Image Strategy:** Product isolated on seamless dark ground, edge-lit so chrome/paint reflections do the talking. Crops are generous rather than aggressive — the object breathes. Every image is treated as a hero, never as a thumbnail.
- **Motion:** GSAP + Barba.js continuous page transitions and Three.js reveals; microinteractions that guide attention. Motion is invisible-when-right: it moves one thing at a time and settles into stillness. No parallax-for-its-own-sake.
- **Spacing:** Very generous — large dark margins around a single subject; blackspace is the primary premium signal.
- **Geometry:** Centered/floated single-object framing with black↔white full-section inversions as rhythm.
- **Mobile Behavior:** One-subject-per-viewport isolation degrades cleanly to stacked full-height sections; the restraint model is inherently mobile-safe.
- **Principle To Apply:** Build the whole system on one dark field, one accent, and disciplined isolation: one hero subject per viewport with real negative space around it, and let After Hourz paint/chrome reflections be the only 'color' event. Use black↔light section inversion as a rhythm device to reset the eye between chapters.
- **What NOT To Copy:** Do not import the cold, EV-engineering neutrality — the neutral grotesque + clinical teal reads futuristic-tech, explicitly off-brand for After Hourz (no futuristic-EV, no generic luxury). Do not lean on Three.js 3D showrooms; that is WebGL-by-default, the opposite of our HTML-first rule. Borrow the restraint, not the tech stack or the coldness.

## Product / Digital

### Apple — AirPods Pro product page

- **URL:** https://www.apple.com/airpods-pro/
- **Screen / Section:** Full-bleed product hero + scroll-driven feature narrative
- **What Works:** Object worship as a design method. Apple builds an entire page around one object rendered with clinical, even lighting and lets whitespace do the persuading. The product is the hero, not the copy; claims are undercut (deflated, made calmer) by breathing room rather than shouted. This is the single most transferable idea for a craft/finish brand: the transformed vehicle IS the product, photographed with the same reverence and given the same negative space. Slow, measured pacing rewards attention instead of demanding it.
- **Composition:** 12-column grid, generous 24-40px gutters. Hero image consumes ~60% of viewport with text claiming the remainder, then alternates full-bleed and contained modules. Each section isolated with 80-140px vertical padding so modules read as punctuation, not a stream. Sections defined by background-color shifts (light to dark) rather than dividers.
- **Typography:** SF Pro Display. Extreme size contrast — hero ~48-64px+ against 16-18px body (roughly 3:1). Headlines use open, airy tracking (+0.5 to +1.5px) — the OPPOSITE of the SaaS negative-tracking trend. Light weights (300-400) for narrative headers, semibold (600) only on CTAs. Hierarchy comes from size and air, not decoration.
- **Image Strategy:** High-fidelity product photography with cool, diffuse, clinical lighting that flatters material (silicone, metal, chip interiors). Alternates full-bleed hero, contained 45-degree/profile shots on subtle gradients, and lifestyle imagery on neutral grounds so the product stays the hero. No warm-sunset drama. Interactive close-look viewer for detail.
- **Motion:** Deliberate and premium, never busy. Slow parallax where the product image lags text on scroll; feature cards fade in as they approach viewport center; a swipe/rotate close-look viewer. Motion is subtle entrance choreography that rewards scrolling, not spectacle.
- **Spacing:** Consistent ~1.5-2x scaling rhythm: headline-to-body ~16-24px, body-to-CTA ~32-48px, section-to-section 80-140px. Mathematically intentional; important sections bracketed by the largest air.
- **Geometry:** Soft, minimal. Card/button radii ~8-12px. No visible borders — separation via soft shadow (0 2px 8px rgba(0,0,0,.1)) or pure whitespace. No dividers, nothing skeuomorphic.
- **Mobile Behavior:** Single-column collapse; hero compresses to ~32-40px but stays dominant; product images go full-width preserving aspect ratio; close-look becomes swipe-driven; section gutters loosen to ~60-80px; hamburger nav with persistent shopping CTA.
- **Principle To Apply:** Treat the finished vehicle as the object of worship: one hero build, reverent even lighting, and enough negative space that the work sells itself. Let SIZE and AIR carry hierarchy so copy can stay quiet and confident — matches the 'precise, patient, understated' brand voice.
- **What NOT To Copy:** Do NOT import Apple's cool, clinical, showroom-white lighting or antiseptic neutrality — that reads as consumer-electronics/EV, explicitly a forbidden drift, and strips the paint of its depth, gloss and controlled-darkness soul. Automotive finish needs warm, raking, reflective light that shows the clearcoat, not flat product-catalog illumination. Also avoid Apple's marketing-superlative claim voice ('world's best'); the brand converts on shown work, not adjectives. Keep the METHOD (object + whitespace + pacing), reject the LIGHTING and the hype register.
- **Relevant Direction:** `all`

Avoid the geometric-sans + tight-negative-tracking headline combo as the primary voice; it reads Silicon Valley, not craft shop. Keep the RESTRAINT DISCIPLINE and the 'one signature' idea; reject the gradient, the tech-UI imagery, and the fintech color story.

### Vercel — homepage

- **URL:** https://vercel.com/
- **Screen / Section:** Centered monochrome hero + 3-column feature blocks
- **What Works:** The most extreme restraint of the four: near-monochrome black/white, one proprietary typeface (Geist), and hierarchy built almost entirely from size and tracking rather than color or weight. Demonstrates that a tightly constrained palette + type system can feel expensive and engineered. The narrow-weight discipline (only 400/500/600, each with a strict role) is a directly stealable governance rule that prevents the 'too many weights' amateur tell.
- **Composition:** Centered column with asymmetric breathing room. Features in 3-column card blocks on a 12-column grid. Whitespace clusters around sections; hero gets maximum air; CTAs bookend the composition. Flat, calm, no decorative fills.
- **Typography:** Geist Sans. Aggressive negative letter-spacing at display sizes (about -2.4px at 48px, scaling: -1.28px at 32px, -0.96px at 24px, normal at 14px) — headlines feel compressed and 'minified.' Strict three-weight system: 400 body/reading, 500 UI/interactive, 600 headings. Hierarchy from size + tracking, NOT weight proliferation.
- **Image Strategy:** Minimal — leans on abstract geometric/technical illustration and monochrome over photography, keeping focus on infrastructure concepts. Accent color reserved strictly for interactive elements.
- **Motion:** Understated: subtle CTA hover (minimal elevation/tone), gentle scroll-triggered card fade-ins, smooth load transitions. Motion never competes with content.
- **Spacing:** Consistent 2:1 / 3:1 spacing multipliers. Hero maximum breathing room; feature cards medium rhythm (~4-6rem gaps); CTAs anchor tight. Isolation reinforces hierarchy.
