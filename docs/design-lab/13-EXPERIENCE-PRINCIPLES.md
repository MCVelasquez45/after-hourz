# 13 — Experience Principles

How the whole thing should *feel* to move through. Doc 03 sets the look; this sets the behavior and
the arc. After Hourz should feel like **craftsmanship translated into software** (doc 04 P1).

## The feeling

Walking into a clean shop at night. Quiet. One finished car under a controlled light. You slow down.
You notice the line of the panel, the depth of the paint. Nobody is selling to you — the work speaks.
Calm, confident, patient, precise.

## The narrative arc (a build, not a brochure)

The experience mirrors the arc of a real job. Sections pace between the three registers (doc 05):

1. **The reveal (hero).** Dark → lit. One statement, one image, enormous restraint. Establishes tone
   and the craft. *Editorial-quiet / cinematic.*
2. **The work (portfolio).** The proof. Real vehicles/process, honestly shown. *Cinematic-band +
   technical-index.*
3. **The craft (process/services).** How the finish happens — layers, patience, precision. Turns
   "services" into a story of transformation. *Technical-index.*
4. **The shop (about/Anthony).** Authentic, grounded, human — not corporate. *Editorial-quiet.*
5. **The ask (contact).** Low-pressure, clear, trustworthy. Everything unknown =
   `CLIENT INPUT REQUIRED`, never faked. *Technical-index.*

(Final IA/pages are a **product-design decision for a later phase** — this is the felt arc, not a
sitemap.)

## Interaction principles

- **Reward attention, don't demand it.** Detail rewards the user who slows down (macro reveals,
  spec plates), but nothing essential is hidden behind an interaction.
- **The interface gets out of the way of the work** (doc 12 / SpaceX). UI recedes; imagery leads.
- **Feedback is immediate and precise** (doc 09 micro-timing). Every interactive thing acknowledges
  you within `dur-micro`.
- **No dark patterns, no pressure.** Trust is the conversion strategy for a craftsman brand.
- **Legible before beautiful.** If a choice trades comprehension for style, comprehension wins
  (doc 04 P7; product-design review, doc 14).

## Accessibility as experience (not compliance theater)

- Fully usable by **keyboard** (visible focus, no traps) and **screen reader** (semantic landmarks,
  real headings, honest alt text describing the work).
- **Reduced-motion** users get an equally coherent, premium experience (doc 09) — not a broken one.
- Touch targets comfortable; contrast meets AA (doc 07/15). Accessibility *is* craftsmanship applied
  to people, which is exactly on-brand.

## Performance as experience

Speed is part of the feeling of quality (doc 04 P9). A premium shop doesn't make you wait; neither
does the site. Fast LCP, no jank, no layout shift. A beautiful screen that stutters has failed.

## Cross-device intent

The experience is **composed** per device (doc 08/16, QA §24), not shrunk. Mobile is its own
cinematic composition (one strong image, big quiet type, thumb-friendly), not "desktop stacked."
Ultra-wide gets air and margin, not stretched text.

## The emotional test (use at every review)

After a pass, ask: *Does this feel like a patient craftsman's night work, or like a business trying
to look expensive?* If the latter, we've decorated instead of designed (doc 04 P3/P10). Strip back.
