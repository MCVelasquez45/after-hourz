# 03 — Art Direction

## The thesis: **"Booth Light"**

A car in a paint booth is the whole brand in one image: a dark, controlled space where a single
disciplined light source reveals the quality of a surface. Nothing decorative. The light exists
only to show the finish. Everything else recedes into black so the *work* is the subject.

That is the site.

> **Booth Light** — near-black space, one controlled light, surfaces that reveal themselves
> slowly. Depth over decoration. The reveal is the payoff.

Three ideas hold it together:

1. **After hours.** The name is literal. This is the patient, obsessive work done when the
   world is quiet — no rush, no audience, just the craft. The experience should feel *nocturnal
   and calm*, not hyped.
2. **The finish.** Custom paint is layers, cure time, wet-sanding, and a reveal. The site should
   have that same sense of depth and of things being *brought up* out of darkness into light.
3. **Precision.** Body work is about true lines and tolerances. The interface should feel
   measured to the pixel — mechanical, exact, confident.

## Emotional tone

Quiet confidence. Anticipation. Respect for the object. The feeling of walking into a clean shop
at night and seeing one finished car under a light — and not needing anyone to explain why it's
good.

## The visual world (mood, not a shopping list)

after-hours craftsmanship · controlled light in darkness · wet, reflective paint · chrome and
polished aluminum · deep near-blacks with real value depth · a single disciplined electric-blue
light source · body lines and quarter-panel geometry · the reveal (dark → lit) · patience and
finishing · Southern California car culture as *authenticity*, never as costume.

## Core moves (the direction's signature gestures)

- **Reveal from black.** Content and imagery emerge from darkness on scroll — like a panel
  coming into the booth light — rather than sliding in decoratively.
- **One light source.** Electric blue behaves like a *light*, not a paint bucket. Used sparingly
  as rim light, focus, and interactive state — never as fields of color.
- **Surface, not ornament.** "Premium" comes from material realism (value depth, specular
  highlights, honest reflection), not from effects layered on top.
- **Editorial restraint.** Big quiet areas of black so one vehicle image can command attention
  without competing UI. (Principle borrowed from Apple/Porsche — see doc 12.)
- **Mechanical precision in layout & motion.** True alignment, tabular numerals, spec-plate
  labels, easing that feels like weighted machinery settling.

## How this beats the automotive-template trap

A generic shop site says "we do paint" with stock icons and a quote form. Booth Light *shows*
the finish and lets restraint do the talking. The differentiation is not a feature — it's the
refusal to decorate, plus real craft in typography, spacing, and material rendering. If we strip
every animation away, the composition and typography must still read as premium (see doc 04,
Principle 7).

## What Booth Light explicitly rejects

Neon glow-everything; flames/checkerboard/lowrider-kitsch as decoration; carbon-fiber
backgrounds; gradients-as-personality; giant meaningless type; 3D because-we-can; a bright,
busy "SoCal" theme. Culture shows up through *authentic subject matter and restraint*, not motifs.
